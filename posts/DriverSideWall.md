After finishing the van capsule interiors last month, I started visualizing how I wanted to build out the driver's side shelving. This would give us usable storage space while adding a nice interior finish to the van.

First, I removed the upper cabinets to open up the space.

![Cabinets removed](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0631.jpg)

Then I added furring strips to create an outer box for the walls, starting with the lower strip and working around the rest of the opening.

![Mounting lower furring strip using plus nuts, marked with hanger screws](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0632.jpg)

![Outer framing](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0638.jpg)

From here, the process was very similar to the van capsule build. I started by building an inner wall box frame on the cutting table, then dry-fit it against the van's inner wall.

![Inner frame on the bench](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0637.jpg)

![Dry fit](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0642.jpg)

Unlike the capsules, this section didn't need a window opening. Instead, I attached a solid piece of 1/4-inch birch plywood to the inner box frame and backed it with a half batt of insulation.

![1/4-inch birch backing](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0640.jpg)

![Sikaflex applied](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0641.jpg)

![Inner box installed](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0648.jpg)

I mounted the assembly, then filled the surrounding gaps with insulation. With the back in place, I built out the outer wall and added the lower shelf.

![Lower shelf framing](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0649.jpg)

![Checking the storage fit](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0650.jpg)

I also wanted an upper shelf, so I added that along with the walls of the inner box.

![Upper shelf framing](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0653.jpg)

Next came scribing the 1/4-inch birch inserts to cover the walls and shelves. Once each piece fit, I removed the individual pieces and applied polyurethane.

![Scribed 1/4-inch liners](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0654.jpg)

![Liners drying](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0656.jpg)

For the outer wall, I used the same approach as the van capsules: mount a sheet of 1/4-inch birch over the framing, then use the router to cut out the shelf openings.

![1/4-inch wall blank](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0657.jpg)

![Router setup](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0659.jpg)

![Routed openings](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0660.jpg)

After coating the wall panel in polyurethane, I mounted it using #6 screws and brad nails.

![Wall panel drying](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0663.jpg)

![First section complete](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0664.jpg)

For the next section, directly behind the driver's seat, I followed the same basic sequence. First came the inner box framing.

![Front section framing](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0684.jpg)

Then I added the rear panel, backed it with insulation, and attached the inner box assembly to the outer framing.

![Insulated back panel](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0687.jpg)

I completed the sides and upper shelves with 3/4-inch birch. This section also got a bottle holder.

![3/4-inch birch shelves and bottle holder](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0691.jpg)

As before, I added 1/4-inch lining pieces to the inside walls and shelves before fitting the outer 1/4-inch wall panel.

![1/4-inch shelf liners](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0698.jpg)

![1/4-inch front wall blank](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0704.jpg)

This part took longer than the first section. The corner of the wall had to be scribed to match the van precisely, so I used a compass and cardboard cutouts to work out the fit.

![Routing in progress](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0706.jpg)

Once the cutting and fitting were done, it was back to polyurethane, including the finger piece for the water bottle holder.

![Front panel and bottle-holder finger piece drying](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0710.jpg)

The finished wall looked even better than the first one. All that practice was starting to show.

![Front section complete](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0711.jpg)

While I was building the wooden shelves, my wife was hand-weaving and knotting custom nets for them. These custom nets really tie all the work together.

![Hand-knotted detail](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0720.jpg)

![Nets fitted](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0719.jpg)

We also wanted a table that could mount against the wall while we were driving, then come down for meals or for my son to do his homework.

That meant taking the upper wall panel off again and adding more furring strips to provide mounting points for the table setup.

![Table mounting supports](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0722.jpg)

We purchased butcher block, cut it to size, and installed collapsible legs. French cleats provide the wall attachment.

![My wife installing the lower French cleat](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0723.jpg)

![Table ready to use](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0725.jpg)

Completing the driver's side wall gives us considerably more storage for toiletries, along with a place for our son to keep his books, toys, and drinks. The butcher-block table is easy to lower for homework, dining, or a game of cards, and just as easy to stow safely when we're done. Beyond making the van more useful, this project brings a significant part of our living space closer to the finished look we've been working toward.

![Completed driver's side wall with birch shelving, hand-knotted nets, reinstalled upper cabinets, and the table stowed](/images/Driver%20Wall/IMG_0729.jpg)
